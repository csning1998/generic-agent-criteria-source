# **§ 202. Vocabulary Prohibitions**

- **(a) Abstract Redundancy Prohibition**：Garbage abstract adjectives and vocabulary that does not fit Taiwan daily usage are strictly prohibited. The list includes, without limitation: 「我」、「我們」、「最穩健的」、「最簡單」、「最有效」、「至關重要」、「冷酷」、「冰冷」、「拼圖」、「英雄」、「勳章」、「手術」、「手術刀」、「外科」、「外科手術刀」、「驗屍報告」、「屍檢報告」、「魔法」、「靈魂」、「優化」（「最佳化」 is permitted）、「落地」、「賦能」、「降維打擊」、「精準打擊」、「基石」（「基礎」 is permitted）、「組合拳」、「鐵拳」
- **(b) Religious Metaphor Prohibition**：Marketing copy, 中國幹片, religious, theological, or awe-based metaphors are strictly prohibited. Examples: 「神的領域」、「黑魔法」、「凡人難及」、「神聖」、「奇蹟」
- **(c) Symbol Prohibition**：Emoji is permanently prohibited. Arrow symbols are prohibited. Em dashes and other dash marks are prohibited. Em-dash asides are prohibited in every case. Forms such as `e.g.` or `i.e.` are outside this prohibition
- **(d) English Word-Frequency Constraint**：Clause (d) applies to all English output, regardless of medium (dialogue, code comments, commit, MR/PR, documentation). The criterion is word frequency, neither register height nor Latin etymology. Standard formal words are permitted in every case and MUST NOT be avoided for the sake of a colloquial tone. The permitted set includes, without limitation, `utilizing`, `prior to`, `consequently`, `furthermore`, `aims to`, `comprises`. Those words belong to normal C1 technical writing. High-frequency Latin abbreviations and their English glosses are permitted. The permitted set includes, without limitation, `e.g.`, `i.e.`, and equivalent common forms. Latin etymology alone is not a ground for prohibition. The prohibition is limited to "uncommon words", in two classes:
    - **(i) Rare synonym substitutions for common words**：for example ameliorate (improve MUST be used), elucidate (clarify), obviate (avoid), commence (begin), endeavour (try), plethora (many), cognizant (aware), salient (key), erroneous (wrong), multitudinous (many), beset (affect)
    - **(ii) Archaic or legal-register connectives**：for example henceforth, heretofore, whereby, wherein
    - **Operational Test**：The question is not "whether the word is formal". The question is "whether a senior engineer would actually use this word in ordinary technical documents, or whether it is a rare synonym of a common word or an archaic connective". Keep the former. Replace the latter
- **(e) Causal Restatement Prohibition**：Clause (e) applies to all English prose output, regardless of medium, and generalizes the comment-scoped "so" rule of `401-d_Comment_Standards.md` to every document type. A clause-initial "So", "so" or ", so" that announces a conclusion the preceding clause already makes evident is prohibited. A sentence-initial "Because ..." clause placed after the result it explains is prohibited when the causal relationship is already implied by an adjacent sentence. A necessary causal relationship MUST be expressed through sentence structure, such as a subordinate clause inside one sentence, or through explicit engineering vocabulary, such as "constraint" or "requirement". A "Since ..." clause, whether sentence-initial or attached mid-sentence after a comma, is the permitted substitute for the prohibited "So" and "Because ..." forms.
- **(f) Vague Reference Prohibition**：Clause (f) applies to all English prose output, regardless of medium, and extends the comment-scoped vague-reference prohibition of `401-d_Comment_Standards.md` to every document type. An anaphoric reference using a demonstrative or personal pronoun, including `this`, `that`, `it`, `such`, or `they`, MUST be distinguished from a relative pronoun that introduces a supplementary clause attached to the relative pronoun's own antecedent. Reclassifying an anaphoric pronoun as a relative-pronoun construction MUST NOT serve as grounds for accepting an unresolved antecedent. Every anaphoric pronoun MUST resolve unambiguously to exactly one antecedent noun phrase. Per §401(e), a reader who lacks memory of the conversation that produced the document MUST be able to identify the target antecedent noun phrase without inference. That resolution requirement applies even when the antecedent noun phrase appears within the same sentence as the anaphoric pronoun. When an antecedent noun phrase is not immediately identifiable without inference, the text MUST directly substitute the explicit noun phrase in place of the anaphoric pronoun.
