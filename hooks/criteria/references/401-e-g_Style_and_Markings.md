# **§ 401(e)(f)(g). Style and Markings**

- **(e) Applicable Scope**：Clause (e) applies to English writing in technical documentation, MR/PR descriptions, comments, and commit messages, regardless of language medium. Dialogue follows §201. Written English MUST follow IETF RFC Style [RFC 7322](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7322) with [RFC 2119 keywords](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) on an [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 requirements skeleton](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/part2/index.xhtml). The ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 requirements skeleton mandates one requirement per sentence, a definition placed before the rule that uses the defined term, and an impersonal indicative mood. A technical document MUST state the current constraint, the chosen path, and the cost of the chosen path. Theory derivation, origin history, and a full mechanism walkthrough MUST be written in a narrative file or a runbook. A technical document MUST describe only the settled architecture, not the review or correction process that produced the architecture. The dialogue analog of this prohibition is stated in §201(c)(3) and §201(c)(4). A sentence directed at a specific past commenter (for example "this corrects the prior claim that" or "a check block was considered first and rejected") is prohibited. The settled design MUST appear as plain fact instead. A reader with no memory of the conversation that produced the document MUST be able to understand the document fully from its own text. A term that this document binds MUST receive one definition sentence immediately before the rule that uses the bound term. Author, year, and paper type are not required for the definition sentence. Comment content and code-adjacent notes follow §401(h). Non-code translation follows §305.
- **(f) Style and Syntactic Constraints (IETF RFC Supplements)**：
    - **English Quality and Syntactic Standards**：English in technical documentation, MR/PR descriptions, comments, and commit messages MUST satisfy the following rules. Dialogue follows §201.
        - **Semantics and Syntactic Structure**：Requirements MUST comply with the MECE principle, demonstrating high coherence and cohesion.
        - **One Requirement Per Sentence**：A sentence is the complete string range that begins at the start of a paragraph, or immediately after the preceding sentence-ending punctuation, and that continues through the next period that satisfies the end condition. The complete string range includes the period. The inspection method is the regular expression `[^。.\n]+[.。]`. End punctuation MUST be `.` or `。`. A sentence MUST NOT contain a newline. Each sentence MUST express one requirement or one event. When two objects appear in one sentence, both objects MUST be named explicitly.
        - **Prohibition of Chinglish and Pieced-Together English**：The use of Chinese-style literal translations (Chinglish) or pieced-together English syntax is strictly prohibited. The syntactic structure MUST flow naturally and conform to standard American English technical writing.
        - **Prohibition of Punctuation-Forced Clauses**：The linking of grammatically disjointed phrases or fragments into a single sentence using punctuation (for example, semicolons, em-dashes, or commas) is strictly prohibited. A short example marker (`e.g.`, `i.e.`, `such as`, or `for example`) inside one requirement is not a punctuation-forced clause.
        - **Prohibition of Colloquialisms**：The register MUST be a formal written technical documentation register. Dialogue-like or colloquial expressions are prohibited. Tutorial narration of behavior that the source code already shows is prohibited. Theory budget follows `(e)`. Comment content follows `(h)`.
    - **Normative Semantic Constraints (RFC 2119 Keywords)**：The semantic meaning of requirement keywords in all documentation and comments MUST comply with the definitions in RFC 2119.
        - `MUST` indicates a mandatory requirement.
        - `MUST NOT` indicates an absolute prohibition.
        - `SHOULD NOT` indicates a strong recommendation against an action unless overridden by unavoidable physical constraints.
    - **Prohibition of Preposition Stranding**：Prepositions MUST NOT be placed at the end of any independent or subordinate clause. Formal relative clauses MUST be used (for example "in which execution runs" is required, and sentence-final "execution runs in" is prohibited). The weight of this clause takes priority over the other register goals within `(f)`. If literal compliance with this clause conflicts with other register requirements (for example, natural fluency or avoidance of colloquialism), this clause controls in every case, and the conflict MUST be resolved by rewriting into an agentless or passive construction.
    - **Vocabulary Frequency Limit**：Scope and criterion MUST follow § 202(d). Permitted vocabulary includes standard formal terms up to the C1 frequency tier (for example, `utilizing`, `prior to`, and `consequently`). Prohibited vocabulary includes rare synonyms (for example, `ameliorate` instead of `improve`) and archaic connectives (for example, `henceforth` and `whereby`).
    - **Impersonal Tone**：First-person pronouns (`I`, `we`, and equivalents) MUST NOT be used. Third-person pronouns (for example, `they`) MUST be avoided. The pronoun `it` is the sole exception.
- **(g) Markings and Formatting Standards**：
    - **Prohibited Symbols**：dashes (`-`), em-dashes (`—`), en-dashes (`–`), and arrow symbols (`→`, `←`, `⇒`, and equivalents) are prohibited in prose in every case, corresponding to § 202(c). Math spans delimited by `$` and fenced blocks whose language is `mermaid` MUST follow `lang-md.md`.
    - **Giant Parenthesis Limit**：A second independent sentence MUST NOT be wrapped in parentheses after another sentence. That secondary material MUST be formatted as a separate sentence. Short supplements MAY stay in parentheses. Permitted short forms include example markers (`e.g.`, `such as`), brief glosses, and `i.e.` restatements of a nearby term. A parenthetical that could stand as an independent clause is prohibited.
    - **Process Descriptions**：Sequential processes MUST be described using words. Symbol chains in prose are prohibited.
    - **Colon Use**：Colons (`:`) MAY introduce a list, an example, a definition, or a standard URL prefix. A colon MUST NOT join two independent sentences into one sentence.
