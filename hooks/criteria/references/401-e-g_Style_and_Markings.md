# **§ 401(e)(f)(g). Style and Markings**

- **(e) Applicable Scope**：This clause applies to English writing in technical documentation, MR/PR descriptions, comments, and commit messages, regardless of language medium. Dialogue follows §201. Written English MUST follow IETF RFC Style [RFC 7322](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7322) with [RFC 2119 keywords](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) on an [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 requirements skeleton](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/part2/index.xhtml). That skeleton is one requirement per sentence, a definition before the rule that uses it, and an impersonal indicative mood. A technical document MUST state the current constraint, the chosen path, and the cost of that path. Theory derivation, origin history, and a full mechanism walkthrough MUST be written in a narrative file or a runbook. A technical document MUST describe only the settled architecture, not the review or correction process that produced it; a sentence directed at a specific past commenter (for example "this corrects the prior claim that" or "a check block was considered first and rejected") is prohibited, and the settled design MUST appear as plain fact instead. A reader with no memory of the conversation that produced the document MUST be able to understand that document fully from its own text. A term that this document binds MUST receive one definition sentence immediately before the rule that uses that term. Author, year, and paper type are not required for that definition. Comment content and code-adjacent notes follow §401(h). Non-code translation follows §305.
- **(f) Style and Syntactic Constraints (IETF RFC Supplements)**：
    - **English Quality and Syntactic Standards**：English in technical documentation, MR/PR descriptions, comments, and commit messages MUST satisfy the following. Dialogue follows §201.
        - **Semantics and Syntactic Structure**：Requirements must comply with the MECE principle, demonstrating high coherence and cohesion.
        - **One Requirement Per Sentence**：A sentence is the complete string range that begins at the start of a paragraph, or immediately after the preceding sentence-ending punctuation, and that continues through the next period which satisfies the end condition. That range includes the period. The inspection method is the regular expression `[^。.\n]+[.。]`. End punctuation is `.` or `。`. A sentence MUST NOT contain a newline. Each sentence MUST express one requirement or one event. When two objects appear in one sentence, those objects MUST be named.
        - **Prohibition of Chinglish and Pieced-Together English**：The use of Chinese-style literal translations (Chinglish) or pieced-together English syntax is strictly prohibited. The syntactic structure must flow naturally and conform to standard American English technical writing.
        - **Prohibition of Punctuation-Forced Clauses**：The linking of grammatically disjointed phrases or fragments into a single sentence using punctuation (e.g. semicolons, em-dashes, or commas) is strictly prohibited. A short example marker (`e.g.`, `i.e.`, `such as`, or `for example`) inside one requirement is not a punctuation-forced clause.
        - **Prohibition of Colloquialisms**：The register must be a formal written technical documentation register. Dialogue-like or colloquial expressions are prohibited. Tutorial narration of behavior that the source already shows is prohibited. Theory budget follows `(e)`. Comment content follows `(h)`.
    - **Normative Semantic Constraints (RFC 2119 Keywords)**：The semantic meaning of requirement keywords in all documentation and comments must comply with the definitions in RFC 2119
        - `MUST` indicates a mandatory requirement.
        - `MUST NOT` indicates an absolute prohibition.
        - `SHOULD NOT` indicates a strong recommendation against an action unless overridden by unavoidable physical constraints
    - **Prohibition of Preposition Stranding**：Prepositions must not be placed at the end of any independent or subordinate clauses. Formal relative clauses must be used (for example "in which ... runs" is required, and sentence-final "runs in" is prohibited). The weight of this clause takes priority over the other register goals within `(f)`. If literal compliance with this clause conflicts with other register requirements (e.g. natural fluency or avoidance of colloquialism), this clause controls in every case, and the conflict MUST be resolved by rewriting into an agentless or passive construction
    - **Vocabulary Frequency Limit**：Scope and criterion follow § 202(d). Permitted Vocabulary is supplemented here (standard formal vocabulary up to the C1 frequency tier, such as `utilizing`, `prior to`, `consequently`) and Prohibited Vocabulary (rare synonyms such as `ameliorate` mapped to `improve`, or archaic connectives such as `henceforth`, `whereby`)
    - **Impersonal Tone**：The use of first-person pronouns (`I`, `we`, and equivalents) is strictly prohibited. The use of third-person pronouns (e.g. `they`) must be avoided. `it` is the sole exception
- **(g) Markings and Formatting Standards**：
    - **Prohibited Symbols**：dashes (`-`), em-dashes (`—`), en-dashes (`–`), and arrow symbols (`→`, `←`, `⇒`, and equivalents) are prohibited in prose in every case, corresponding to § 202(c). Math spans delimited by `$` and fenced blocks whose language is `mermaid` follow `lang-md.md`.
    - **Giant Parenthesis Limit**：A second independent sentence MUST NOT be wrapped in parentheses after another sentence. That material MUST be a separate sentence. Short supplements MAY stay in parentheses. Permitted short forms include examples (`e.g.`, `such as`), brief glosses, and `i.e.` restatements of a nearby term. A parenthetical that could stand as an independent clause is prohibited.
    - **Process Descriptions**：Sequential processes MUST be described with words. Symbol chains in prose are prohibited
    - **Colon Use**：Colons (`:`) MAY introduce a list, an example, a definition, or a standard URL prefix. A colon MUST NOT join two independent sentences into one.
